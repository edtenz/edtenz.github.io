A Case to Consider

The Ovarian Lottery

Warren Buffett has described his success partly through what he calls the "ovarian lottery" — being born male, white, in the United States, in 1930, with certain cognitive abilities and a father who worked in finance.

He once estimated: "If I'd been born in Bangladesh, or born here 200 years earlier, my particular talents would have been useless."

At the same time, Buffett started reading financial statements at age 11, filed his first tax return at 13, and compounded knowledge relentlessly for seven decades.

Neither the starting conditions nor the effort alone seems sufficient to explain the outcome. What's interesting is how they interact.

Closing

"I am a great believer in luck, and I find the harder I work, the more I have of it."

— Often attributed to various figures; earliest similar phrasing by Coleman Cox, 1922

Maybe the most honest answer is: both matter, but they don't matter equally for everyone. Recognizing luck doesn't erase effort — it just makes space for empathy.
