THREE WORDS YOU NEED TODAY

Three Concepts, One Question

🛒

Consumerism

消费主义
The culture does it.

A way of life where buying is not just about use, but about identity, status, reward, and belonging.

⚡

Impulse Buying

冲动消费
The moment does it.

You buy quickly because of emotion, social pressure, discounts, scarcity, or a persuasive recommendation.

🧘

Minimalism

极简生活
The boundary does it.

Owning fewer things on purpose, so your money, space, time, and attention serve what matters more.

Today's core question: Are we satisfying needs, or manufacturing new desires?

ROUND 1 · 4 QUESTIONS · 25 MIN

Recognizing Your Own Habits

Q1
The Last Purchase Test

Think of the last non-essential thing you bought. What triggered it: need, discount, mood, recommendation, boredom, or social pressure?

Q2
Online Shopping vs. Real Stores

Where are you more likely to buy things you didn't plan to buy: Taobao, JD, Xiaohongshu, Douyin livestreams, or offline stores? Why?

Q3
Brands and Identity

Is there a brand, gadget, clothing style, or product category that says something about who you are? Is that expression real, performed, or both?

Q4
Regret and Satisfaction

What purchase gave you long-term satisfaction? What purchase quickly became clutter（杂物/闲置品）? What was the difference?

Take turns. Everyone answers Q1 first, then move deeper. About 6 minutes per question.

BEFORE ROUND 2 · THE TENSION

Need, desire, or anxiety?

A purchase can be practical, emotional, social, and symbolic at the same time. The hard part is knowing which force is driving.

🔧

Need

It solves a real problem: food, health, work, safety, replacement, convenience, or quality of life.

✨

Desire

It gives pleasure, beauty, identity, novelty, status, comfort, or a sense of possibility.

⏳

Anxiety

It promises: buy this before you fall behind, look outdated, miss the sale, or become less attractive.

Round 2 is not about judging people. It is about seeing the system more clearly.

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 1

Is consumerism mostly freedom or manipulation?

Online shopping gives us choice and convenience. But it also gives companies endless chances to shape our attention, emotions, and self-image.

IT'S FREEDOM

  • People can choose what makes their life better
  • Good products save time, energy, and stress
  • Shopping can support creativity, hobbies, and self-expression

IT'S MANIPULATION

  • Algorithms learn our weak moments better than we do
  • Scarcity, discounts, and influencers create artificial urgency（人为制造的紧迫感）
  • Brands sell identity, not just function

Discussion tip: defend the side you disagree with for two minutes.

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 2 · ROLE-PLAY

Who benefits from our desire?

Split into 4 mini-groups. Each defends one role. 5 min prep, 10 min cross-debate.

📱

PLATFORMS

(Taobao, Douyin, Xiaohongshu...)

Should platforms reduce manipulative design（操纵性设计） even if sales and engagement drop?

📣

BRANDS & INFLUENCERS

(marketing, KOLs, live hosts)

Is emotional selling a problem, or just good communication?

🏛️

GOVERNMENT

(regulators, consumer protection)

Should there be stronger rules on ads, data use, dark patterns（暗黑设计模式）, or "lowest price" claims?

🙋

EACH OF US

(consumers, friends, families)

How much responsibility should individuals take for resisting temptation?

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 3 · CLOSING ACTION

What would a healthier relationship with buying look like?

Minimalism is not the only answer. The deeper question is: can we buy with more awareness, less anxiety, and more respect for future ourselves?

IDEAS TO STEAL:

  • Wait 24 hours before buying non-essential items
  • Ask: "What problem will this still solve one month from now?"
  • Unfollow one account that makes you feel inadequate
  • Track one week of small purchases without judging yourself
  • Repair, borrow, rent, or buy second-hand once this month

Finish with: "This week, I will ___."
