THREE WORDS YOU NEED TODAY

Three Concepts, One Question

🛒

Consumerism

消费主义
The culture does it.

A way of life where buying is not just about use, but about identity, status, reward, and belonging.

⚡

Impulse Buying

冲动消费
The moment does it.

You buy quickly because of emotion, social pressure, discounts, scarcity, or a persuasive recommendation.

🧘

Minimalism

极简生活
The boundary does it.

Owning fewer things on purpose, so your money, space, time, and attention serve what matters more.

FOUR SIDES OF THE SAME COIN

What's actually happening when we buy?

01
Solving Problems

Better tools, faster delivery, wider access — buying genuinely improves quality of life for most people.

02
Expressing Identity

What we buy signals who we are — or who we want to be. Brands, styles, and choices tell a story about us.

03
Seeking Emotion

The unboxing dopamine, the "treat yourself" moment, retail therapy — buying IS a feeling, not just a transaction.

04
Responding to Design

Countdown timers, "only 3 left," personalized recommendations — platforms are very good at making "later" feel like "now."

None of these is inherently good or bad. The question is: which one drives YOUR buying — and do you notice?

ROUND 1 · 4 QUESTIONS · 25 MIN

Recognizing Your Own Habits

Q1
The Last Purchase Test

Think of the last non-essential thing you bought. What triggered it: need, discount, mood, recommendation, boredom, or social pressure?

Q2
Online Shopping vs. Real Stores

Where are you more likely to buy things you didn't plan to buy: Taobao, JD, Xiaohongshu, Douyin livestreams, or offline stores? Why?

Q3
Brands and Identity

Is there a brand, gadget, clothing style, or product category that says something about who you are? Is that expression real, performed, or both?

Q4
Regret and Satisfaction

What purchase gave you long-term satisfaction? What purchase quickly became clutter（杂物/闲置品）? What was the difference?

Take turns. Everyone answers Q1 first, then move deeper. About 6 minutes per question.

BEFORE ROUND 2 · GOING DEEPER

Three reasons we buy — often at the same time.

Most purchases serve more than one purpose. The interesting question is which force you notice — and which operates silently.

🔧

Function

It solves a real problem: saves time, replaces something broken, makes daily life smoother.

✨

Meaning

It says something about who you are: taste, values, aspiration. You're not just buying the thing — you're buying the story.

🎭

Moment

It feels right NOW: the mood, the discount, the social proof, the dopamine. Timing is a reason too.

Seeing

3 questions · switch groups · go from personal habits to social consequences

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 1

Why do we buy things we didn't plan to buy?

Unplanned purchases aren't always "mistakes." Sometimes they're discoveries. But the system is designed to create them. Explore both sides:

🎯 IT'S DISCOVERY

  • Recommendations help you find things you'd never search for
  • Spontaneity（自发性）is part of a good life
  • Not all unplanned = regretted — some are your best purchases

🎪 IT'S BY DESIGN

  • Platforms profit when you buy on impulse — their UX is optimized for it
  • "Only 2 left" and countdown timers bypass rational thinking
  • Emotional states (bored, sad, celebrating) are targetable moments

Think of YOUR last unplanned purchase. Was it a happy discovery or a designed moment? Can it be both?

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 2 · ROLE-PLAY

Who shapes how we spend?

Split into 4 roles. Each group explains their perspective. 5 min prep, 10 min cross-discussion.

📱

PLATFORMS

(Taobao, Douyin, Xiaohongshu...)

"We connect buyers with what they want faster. If people regret purchases, is that our fault or theirs?"

📣

CREATORS & BRANDS

(KOLs, live hosts, marketing)

"We tell stories about products. Storytelling isn't lying — it's helping people imagine a better life."

🙋

CONSUMERS

(that's us)

"We're adults. We make choices. But... how free is a choice when the whole environment is designed to nudge（助推） us?"

🔬

RESEARCHERS

(behavioral science, economics)

"Humans are predictably irrational（可预见的非理性）. The question isn't IF we're influenced — it's whether that's acceptable."

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 3 · PERSONAL EXPERIMENT

What would you change — if anything?

There's no "correct" way to spend. But after today's discussion — is there ONE thing you'd try differently this week? Not to be "better," just to notice more.

SMALL EXPERIMENTS TO TRY:

  • Wait 24 hours before buying something non-essential — see if you still want it
  • Track one week of small purchases, no judgment — just data
  • Before buying, ask: "Am I solving a problem, expressing myself, or responding to a mood?"
  • Try buying nothing for fun for 3 days — notice what changes (and what doesn't)

Share with the group: "This week, I'm curious to try ___."
