THREE WORDS YOU NEED TODAY

Three Concepts, One Trade-off

👣

Digital Footprint

数字足迹
Every click leaves a trace.

The trail of data you create online: searches, purchases, locations, likes, messages — even how long you pause on a video.

🔍

Surveillance Economy

监控经济
Your attention is the product.

A business model where companies profit by collecting, analyzing, and selling data about human behavior at scale.

📋

Informed Consent

知情同意
Do you really agree?

The idea that you should understand what data is collected and how it's used before you say "I agree" — but do you ever read the terms?

FROM DATA TO DAILY LIFE

What do these numbers mean for you?

01
Personalization

Algorithms know your taste in music, food, news, and shopping. They save you time — but also build a profile you never agreed to.

02
Normalization（常态化）

Face scanning to enter a gym, real-name registration for every app, location sharing to order food — it all feels "normal" now.

03
Asymmetry（不对称）

Companies know almost everything about you. You know almost nothing about what they do with your data. The relationship is one-sided.

04
Irreversibility（不可逆性）

Once data is collected, it's nearly impossible to take back. A leaked database stays leaked. A face scan stays in the system.

Insight: Convenience trains us not to ask questions. The smoother the experience, the less we notice what we give up.

BEFORE ROUND 2 · THE DEEPER TENSION

Convenience, control, or something else?

Most of us accept the deal without thinking. But the deal is not just "data for service" — it shapes how we're seen, judged, and governed.

🎯

Convenience

It saves time, removes friction, and makes life smoother. One-click checkout, smart routes, instant ID verification.

🏗️

Control

Data gives power to those who hold it: to predict, influence, restrict, or reward. Who decides how your profile is used?

🫥

Erosion（侵蚀）

Privacy loss is gradual. No single app feels dangerous. But together, they create a picture of you that you never authorized.

Round 2 goes beyond personal habits — it asks who benefits, and what kind of world we're building.

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 1

Is "I have nothing to hide" a good argument?

Many people say: "I don't do anything illegal, so why should I care about privacy?" But privacy is not just about hiding wrongdoing. Consider:

PRIVACY IS A RIGHT

  • You close the bathroom door not because you're hiding a crime
  • Self-censorship（自我审查）: if you know you're watched, you act differently
  • Data can be used against you in contexts you never imagined
  • Power imbalance（权力不对等）: they know everything; you know nothing

CONVENIENCE IS WORTH IT

  • Most data collection genuinely improves services
  • Crime prevention and public safety depend on surveillance
  • Total privacy can mean total inefficiency
  • Younger generations may define privacy differently

Discussion tip: try to explain WHY you feel the way you do, not just what you believe.

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 2 · ROLE-PLAY

Who benefits most from our data?

Split into 4 mini-groups. Each defends one role. 5 min prep, 10 min cross-debate.

🏢

TECH COMPANIES

(Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance...)

We use data to give users better, faster, more personalized experiences. Without data, innovation stops.

🏛️

GOVERNMENT

(public safety, urban management)

Surveillance keeps cities safe and services efficient. Citizens benefit from order and security.

🙋

INDIVIDUAL USERS

(you, your friends, your family)

We get free services and convenience — but at what long-term cost? Do we truly have a choice?

👶

FUTURE GENERATIONS

(children born into this system)

They never consented to a world of total data collection. What digital environment are we leaving them?

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 3 · CLOSING ACTION

What would a healthier relationship with data look like?

Going off-grid is not realistic. The real question is: can we use technology with more awareness, more choice, and less blind trust?

IDEAS TO STEAL:

  • Review your app permissions this weekend — revoke（撤销） anything unnecessary
  • Ask: "What data does this app need vs. what it's actually collecting?"
  • Use the "ask every time" setting for location, instead of "always allow"
  • Read one privacy policy this month — just to see what you've been agreeing to
  • Talk to someone older or younger about privacy — compare your boundaries

Finish with: "After today's discussion, I will ___."
