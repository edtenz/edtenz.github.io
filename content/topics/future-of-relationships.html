FROM DATA TO DAILY LIFE

What do these numbers hide?

01
Convenience vs. Depth

Apps make it easy to find people — but swiping, matching, and chatting rarely lead to deep connection. Starting is easy; staying is hard.

02
The Buddy Economy

Young Chinese increasingly prefer low-pressure, function-based connections: meal buddies, gym buddies, travel buddies. Efficient — but where does intimacy（亲密感） live?

03
AI That Listens

AI companions never judge, never leave, and never get tired. For some, that fills a real gap. For others, it replaces the motivation to connect with humans.

04
Remote Everything

Remote work, online classes, food delivery, live-streaming — modern life is designed so you never NEED to see another person. Convenience becomes isolation（便利变成隔离）.

Sources: iResearch China Social Networking Report, 2024; MIT Technology Review on AI Companionship, 2024; People's Daily on "搭子文化," 2024.

ROUND 1 · 4 QUESTIONS · 25 MIN

How We Connect Now

Q1
The New Friend Test

How did you meet your most recent new friend or connection? Was it through an app, an event, work, a mutual friend, or pure chance? How long did it take to feel real?

Q2
Buddy: Friend or Function?

Do you have a "buddy" — for the gym, meals, or studying? Is that friendship, or just convenience? Can it become something deeper?

Q3
Closer Online?

Have you ever felt closer to someone you only know online than to people you see every day? What made that digital connection feel real — or was it an illusion?

Q4
The Low-Cost Default

Sending a meme, liking a post, leaving a short voice message — is this "maintaining a relationship" or just the appearance of one? Where is the line?

Take turns. Everyone answers Q1 first, then move deeper. About 6 minutes per question.

BEFORE ROUND 2 · THE TENSION

What are we optimizing for?

Technology gives us options. But every design choice shapes how we relate to each other. What are we gaining — and what might we be losing?

⚡

Efficiency

Find people faster, filter by interest, skip awkward beginnings. But deep bonds are built slowly — through friction, not filters.

🛡️

Safety

Control who you talk to, when, and how much. Set boundaries easily. But avoiding risk also means avoiding vulnerability（脆弱/坦诚）.

♾️

Optionality

Infinite choices of people, communities, and connections. But abundance can make us treat people as replaceable（可替换的）.

Round 2 is not anti-technology. It asks: what are we willing to trade?

ROUND 2 · 25 MIN

What Are
We Trading?

3 questions · switch groups · from personal habits to societal trade-offs

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 1

Is AI companionship helping or replacing human connection?

Millions of people now talk to AI for emotional support, practice conversations, or just feel less alone. Is this a bridge back to humans — or a comfortable exit from the hard work of real relationships?

IT HELPS

  • Gives lonely people a safe space to practice being open
  • Available 24/7 — no scheduling, no judgment, no rejection
  • Can supplement（补充） human relationships, not replace them

IT REPLACES

  • Removes the motivation to tolerate real human imperfection
  • Creates an illusion of intimacy without any real vulnerability
  • The easier the substitute, the less we invest in the real thing

Discussion tip: Would you feel comfortable if your best friend told you their closest relationship was with an AI?

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 2

Is "buddy culture" making socializing healthier — or shallower?

Buddy culture means finding someone for a specific activity — eating, exercising, studying, traveling — with low emotional investment. It is efficient and pressure-free. But can a society function on convenience-based bonds alone?

HEALTHIER
Low Pressure

No obligation to share your life story. You enjoy the activity, not perform intimacy. Less social anxiety.

HEALTHIER
Clear Expectations

Both sides know the scope. No disappointment, no drama. Boundaries are built in from the start.

SHALLOWER
Disposable Connections

If the function ends, the relationship ends. People become interchangeable（可互换的）. No roots grow.

SHALLOWER
Avoiding Vulnerability

Deep relationships require risk: being seen, being rejected, being disappointed. Buddy culture lets you opt out.

Discuss: Is a buddy a stepping stone to deeper friendship — or a substitute for it?

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 3 · CLOSING REFLECTION

What will relationships look like in 10 years?

Imagine yourself in 2035. AI is smarter, remote work is normal, social apps have evolved again. What do you hope your relationships will look like — and what are you afraid they might become?

FUTURE SCENARIOS TO DISCUSS:

  • Will people choose AI companions over imperfect human partners?
  • Will "buddies" become the default relationship model for most social needs?
  • Will in-person time become a luxury — expensive, rare, and highly valued?
  • Will loneliness become a public health crisis that governments must address?
  • Will we develop new social skills — or lose the ones we used to have?

Finish with: "In 10 years, I hope my relationships will ___."
