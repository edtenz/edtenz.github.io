THREE WORDS YOU NEED TODAY

Three Concepts, One Question

👁️

Attention Economy

注意力经济
The system does it.

A marketplace where human attention is the scarce resource being harvested, traded, and sold to advertisers.

🕳️

Doomscrolling

无意识刷屏
The habit does it.

Compulsively scrolling through bad news or endless feeds, unable to stop even though it makes you feel worse.

🎯

Deep Work

深度工作
The discipline does it.

Sustained, focused effort on a cognitively demanding task — the opposite of fragmented attention.

FROM DATA TO DAILY LIFE

What do these numbers hide?

01
Convenience

We get news, entertainment, learning, and connection instantly — more accessible than any time in history.

02
Design for Addiction（成瘾设计）

Infinite scroll, autoplay, push notifications, and variable rewards（可变奖励）are designed to keep you engaged, not informed.

03
Fragmented Thinking（碎片化思维）

A 2024 study in Nature found that heavy social media users showed reduced ability to sustain attention（持续注意力）and filter irrelevant information.

04
Emotional Cost（情绪代价）

Constant stimulation leaves less room for boredom — which is where creativity, reflection, and deep thinking begin.

Sources: Nature Human Behaviour, 2024; Tristan Harris, Center for Humane Technology; Cal Newport, "Deep Work," 2016.

BEFORE ROUND 2 · THE TENSION

Distraction, design, or dependence?

Every scroll has three layers. Understanding them helps us see beyond personal willpower.

🎰

Distraction

Your brain seeks novelty and reward. Short videos and feeds deliver micro-doses of dopamine（多巴胺）with zero effort.

🧲

Design

Tech companies hire thousands of engineers to make products as engaging as possible. Your weakness is their KPI.

🔗

Dependence

When information, socializing, work, and entertainment all live on one device, opting out becomes nearly impossible.

Round 2 is not about blaming yourself. It is about seeing the system.

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 1

Is the attention economy stealing our ability to think deeply?

We have more information than ever, yet many people feel less able to concentrate, read long texts, or sit with complex ideas.

YES — IT'S DAMAGING

  • Constant switching trains the brain to seek shallow rewards
  • We read headlines but rarely finish articles
  • Young people report difficulty reading books they once loved
  • Complex problems require sustained thought — scrolling trains the opposite

NO — WE'RE ADAPTING

  • Humans have always had distractions; we adjust
  • Short-form content can spark curiosity and lead to deeper exploration
  • We now process more diverse information faster
  • The real issue is education, not technology

Discussion tip: share a personal example before arguing a position.

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 2

What does fragmented information do to us?

We now consume more information in fragments than ever before. Is this a net gain, a hidden loss, or simply a trade-off? Consider these four effects and discuss where you stand.

🌊 01
Breadth vs. Depth

We know a little about many things, but rarely go deep. This makes us versatile in conversation — but does it also make our understanding shallow and our opinions borrowed?

⚡ 02
Speed vs. Accuracy

We learn about events in seconds — faster than any generation before. But speed often means missing context. Does getting news fast make us more informed or more reactive?

🌐 03
Access vs. Overload

Anyone can now learn almost anything for free. But when there's too much, we either skim everything or freeze and choose nothing. Is unlimited access still a gift?

🪞 04
Connection vs. Echo

Fragments travel fast and connect people around shared topics. But algorithms show us what we already agree with. Are we broadening our world — or just hearing our own voice louder?

No right answer. Pick 1–2 that resonate with your experience and share a real example.

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 3 · CLOSING ACTION

How do we take our attention back?

Going offline completely is not realistic. The question is: can we be intentional about where our attention goes, instead of letting algorithms decide?

IDEAS TO STEAL:

  • Set a daily screen time limit for your top 3 apps
  • Turn off all non-human notifications (keep messages, mute marketing)
  • Try "single-tasking" for one hour: one tab, one task, phone face-down
  • Replace 10 minutes of scrolling with 10 minutes of reading or walking
  • Ask before opening an app: "What am I looking for?" — if no answer, don't open it

Finish with: "This week, I will try ___."
