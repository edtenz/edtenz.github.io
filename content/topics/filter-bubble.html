THREE WORDS YOU NEED TODAY

Three Concepts, One Phenomenon

🫧

Filter Bubble

过滤气泡
The algorithm does it.

Coined by Eli Pariser (2011). Algorithms quietly filter what you see, based on clicks, location, history.

🔊

Echo Chamber

回音室
Your social circle does it.

Your beliefs are amplified by like-minded people. Disagreement is filtered out — by your community.

🐛

Information Cocoon

信息茧房
You do it to yourself.

Cass Sunstein (2006). You wrap yourself in your own preferences, avoiding what challenges you.

Today we'll use "information cocoon" as the umbrella term — but notice which kind YOU are in.

A CLASSIC EXAMPLE

Two friends. One search. Two realities.

In 2010, both searched Google for "BP" on the same day:

Friend A saw:

📈 BP stock up 2.1%
💼 BP investment opportunities
📰 BP quarterly earnings beat

Friend B saw:

🌊 Deepwater Horizon oil spill
🐦 Pelicans coated in oil
⚖️ BP faces criminal charges

Same search. Same engine. Same instant. Why? Because Google had already decided who they were.

Recognizing Your Own Bubble

Q1
The "Same App, Different World" Test

Open Douyin or Xiaohongshu now. Compare your top 3 videos with the person next to you. Are they similar or completely different? What does that tell you?

Q2
Your Algorithm Knows You

What's something the algorithm "figured out" about you that even your friends or family don't know? Was it accurate? A little creepy?

Q3
The Last Time You Disagreed

When did you last see an opinion online you strongly disagreed with? Did you engage, ignore, or block it? What does your answer say about your information diet?

Q4
Comparing Platforms

Where do you feel most "trapped" — Douyin, Xiaohongshu, Weibo, or Bilibili? Why? Are some platforms more echo-chamber-y than others?

Take turns — each person answers Q1 before moving on. About 6 minutes per question.

BEFORE ROUND 2 · THE STAKES

This isn't just about cat videos.

When information cocoons go wrong, real things break.

🗳️

Brexit & US 2016 Election

Two halves of the same country lived in different information realities — and were genuinely shocked by election results.

💉

Vaccine Misinformation（疫苗虚假信息）

During COVID-19, anti-vaccine communities reinforced each other in tight online circles, contributing to lower vaccination rates and deaths.

⚠️

Online Radicalization（网络极端化）

Echo chambers like incel forums escalated isolated frustrations into extremist ideologies（极端主义思想） — and, in some cases, real violence.

Sources: Pariser (2011), Loomba et al. on COVID misinformation, Fondation Descartes

ROUND 2 · QUESTION 1

Is It Really a Problem?

Some researchers argue filter bubbles are exaggerated — humans have ALWAYS preferred news that fits their views. Is the "information cocoon" a genuinely new crisis, or just a new name for an old human habit?

👀 IT'S A REAL CRISIS

  • Algorithms are far more personalized than newspapers ever were
  • Speed and scale are unprecedented（前所未有的）
  • Most people don't even know they're in a bubble

🤔 IT'S OVERHYPED

  • Studies show people actually see MORE diverse views online
  • Most users follow some accounts they disagree with
  • The real cause is human bias（人的认知偏差）, not the algorithm

Discussion tip: Try defending the side you DON'T agree with. That's the whole point.
