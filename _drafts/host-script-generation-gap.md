# Host Script: Generation Gap

**Topic:** Do Your Parents Get You? — Generation Gap, Value Shifts & The World Between Us  
**Duration:** 90 minutes  
**Structure:** 2 rounds, 7 questions, 8 open cards  
**Ideal group size:** 15-30 participants

## Before the Session

### Room Setup (arrive 15 min early)

- Test projector/screen, advance slides once to confirm navigation works
- Arrange chairs in clusters of 5-7 (you will use these for both rounds)
- Place name tags and markers on each table (optional but helpful)
- Have a timer visible on your phone or laptop

### Your Mindset

- You are a facilitator, not a lecturer. Your job is to create space for others to talk.
- 你是引导者，不是老师。今晚的主角是参与者，不是你。
- Energy comes from YOU in the first 10 minutes, then from the GROUP. Front-load your enthusiasm.

---

## Slide 1 — Title Slide

**Time:** 0:00-0:30 (30 seconds)  
**Action:** Display as people settle in. Stand at the front, smile, make eye contact.

> **SAY:**  
> "Welcome, everyone! Tonight's topic is one that every single person in this room has experienced. Take a look at the screen — 'Do Your Parents Get You?' — and we're going to talk about that space between generations. Please find a seat with people you don't already know if possible."

**Tip:** Don't rush to start. Let people sit down and get comfortable. Chat casually with early arrivals.  
/ 不要急着开始。让大家坐好。跟早到的人随便聊几句暖场。

---

## Slide 2 — Ice Breaker

**Time:** 0:30-5:30 (5 minutes)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 2. Energize your voice.

> **SAY:**  
> "Alright! Let's start with introductions. This is super simple. Turn to the person next to you — or your whole table — and share three things."

*Point to the screen and read each card:*

> "Number one: your name — 'Hi, I'm ___. You can call me ___.'"  
> "Number two: what you do — 'I work as a ___' or 'I'm studying ___.'"  
> "And number three — this is the fun one — finish this sentence: 'One thing my parents always say is ___.'"

*Give an example of your own:*

> "I'll go first. I'm [your name], I [your job]. And one thing my parents always say is '**[your real example — e.g., "Don't stay up so late!" / "When are you getting married?"]**'"

> "OK, go! You have about one minute per person. Start now."

**Facilitation:**
- Walk around. Listen. Laugh with them.
- If a table is quiet, sit down with them and ask "Who wants to go first?"
- At 4:30, give a 30-second warning: "Wrap up your last introduction!"
- / 走动。倾听。如果某桌很安静，坐下来问"谁先来？"

**Handling quiet groups:** Physically join the quiet table. Share your own answer first. Ask directly: "What about you? What does YOUR mom always say?" Direct questions are not rude here — they are permission to speak.

## Slide 3 — Warm-Up: The 10,000 Yuan Question

**Time:** 5:30-8:30 (3 minutes)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 3.

> **SAY:**  
> "OK! Now a quick thought experiment. Imagine someone just hands you 10,000 RMB — no strings attached, totally free money."

*Pause. Let them read the slide.*

> "The question is simple: what would YOU do with it? And what would your PARENTS do with the exact same money?"

*Point to the two columns on screen:*

> "I bet most of you fall somewhere on the left — trips, gadgets, courses. And your parents? Probably the right side — save it, health stuff, or 'put it aside for the grandkids.'"

> "Quick pair-share with the person next to you: whose instinct do you think is actually wiser? 30 seconds. Go!"

*After 30 seconds:*

> "Same money. Same family. Completely different priorities. And THAT is what we're talking about tonight. This gap — where does it come from? Can we bridge it? Should we?"

**Tip:** This is a low-stakes warm-up. Don't over-discuss. Its purpose is to make the topic concrete and personal in 3 minutes.  
/ 这只是热身。不要过度讨论。目的是让话题变得具体和个人化。

---

## Slide 4 — Three Concepts

**Time:** 8:30-12:00 (3.5 minutes)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 4. Shift to a slightly more "teaching" tone — but keep it conversational.

> **SAY:**  
> "Before we dive into discussion, let me give you three terms — three lenses for looking at this topic. You'll see them on the screen."

*Point to each card:*

> "First: **Generation Gap** — 代沟. This is the basic idea: people who grew up in different decades literally grew up in different worlds. Your grandparents grew up during the Cultural Revolution. Your parents grew up during Reform and Opening Up. You grew up with smartphones. Same country, different universes."

> "Second: **Value Shift** — 价值观转变. As society changes, beliefs follow. What your parents thought was 'obvious' — get married by 25, buy an apartment, have kids — those aren't obvious to you at all. It's not rebellion. It's just... a different world produces different priorities."

> "Third: **Digital Divide** — 数字鸿沟. Technology doesn't just create new tools — it creates separate realities. If your parents can't use WeChat Pay, they experience the world differently from you. If they get their news from CCTV and you get yours from Bilibili or Xiaohongshu — you're not even seeing the same reality."

*Pause.*

> "So here's our core question for tonight — and I want you to keep this in the back of your mind: **Why can the same event mean completely different things to different generations?** A wedding, a job change, a move abroad — same event, totally different emotional reactions depending on when you were born."

**Tip:** Speak slowly on the three terms. Let people read along. If your audience has lower English proficiency, repeat each term and its Chinese translation clearly.  
/ 三个概念说慢一点。让大家跟着读。如果水平参差不齐，中英文都念清楚。

---

## Slide 5 — Statistics

**Time:** 12:00-14:30 (2.5 minutes)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 5. Let the numbers land visually before you speak.

> **SAY:**  
> "Now let me hit you with some numbers. Take a moment to look at the screen."

*Pause 5 seconds. Then walk through each stat:*

> "28.7 — that's the average age of first marriage in China in 2022. In 1990 it was 22.4. That's a six-year shift in one generation."

> "61% of Gen Z workers change jobs within two years. For our parents' generation? Only 12%. They stayed in one company for decades."

> "Only 36% of people born after 1995 think marriage is 'necessary.' For people born before 1970, it was 78%. That's not a small gap — that's a fundamentally different view of life."

> "And 52% of people over 60 use mobile payment. Sounds high, right? But it's 93% for people under 30. Forty percent of our grandparents are living in a different financial infrastructure."

> "These aren't just statistics. These represent completely different ways of seeing life, work, love, and money."

**Tip:** Don't rush through numbers. Give each one a beat. Let the audience react ("Wow" / nodding / whispering). Numbers create shared surprise — that builds group energy.  
/ 数字不要念太快。每个给一拍。观众会有反应——这种共同的惊讶感是凝聚力的来源。

---

## Slide 6 — Four Tensions

**Time:** 14:30-17:00 (2.5 minutes)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 6.

> **SAY:**  
> "So what do all those numbers actually mean in daily life? I think the gap shows up as four big tensions."

*Point to each quadrant:*

> "**Scarcity vs. Abundance.** Our parents grew up saving every cent because they had to. We grew up knowing the economy would keep growing — at least, we thought it would. Same family, opposite instincts about risk."

> "**Duty vs. Choice.** For older generations, marriage, career, children — these are duties. For younger generations, they're choices. Or even optional. That difference creates enormous friction at family dinners."

> "**Stability vs. Freedom.** A 'good life' used to mean one thing: stable job, apartment, family. Now it might mean freelancing from Bali, or simply not being bored. Try explaining that to someone who lived through the 90s layoffs."

> "**Respect vs. Authenticity.** Parents value harmony, face, and obedience. Children value honesty, self-expression, and boundaries. And here's the thing — both sides call what they do 'love.'"

> "These aren't right-or-wrong opposites. They're value systems shaped by completely different economic realities. Keep these in mind as we go into Round 1."

**Tip:** This slide is transitional. Don't over-explain. You're building a bridge from context to conversation.  
/ 过渡页。不要解释太多。你在搭桥——从背景信息到讨论环节。

---

## Slide 7 — Round 1 Title

**Time:** 17:00-17:30 (30 seconds)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 7. Raise your energy.

> **SAY:**  
> "Alright! Time to talk. Round 1 — 'Recognizing the Gap.' Twenty-five minutes. Four questions. You'll discuss in your current table groups of 5 to 7."

> "The rules are simple: take turns, everyone speaks, and we start from REAL experiences — not theory. I'll keep time and move you along. Ready? Let's go."

## Slide 8 — Round 1 Questions (Q1-Q4)

**Time:** 17:30-42:30 (25 minutes)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 8. This is the CORE discussion. Your role shifts from speaker to facilitator.

> **SAY:**  
> "Here are your four questions. You can see them all on the screen. Let me read them quickly."

*Read each question title and the first sentence:*

> "Q1 — The Money Test. How do your parents feel about how you spend money?"  
> "Q2 — The Career Conversation. Do your parents understand what you do for a living?"  
> "Q3 — The Love Question. At what age do your parents think you should marry?"  
> "Q4 — The Tech Divide. Have you ever had to teach your parents how to use technology?"

> "Start with Q1. Everyone shares on Q1 first, then move to Q2, and so on. About 6 minutes per question. You probably won't get to all four — that's fine. Go deep, not wide."

> "I'll give you a 'switch' signal every 6 minutes. Start now!"

### Timing breakdown:

| Time | Action |
|------|--------|
| 17:30-23:30 | Q1 discussion (6 min) — announce "Move to Q2!" |
| 23:30-29:30 | Q2 discussion (6 min) — announce "Move to Q3!" |
| 29:30-35:30 | Q3 discussion (6 min) — announce "Q4 — last one!" |
| 35:30-41:30 | Q4 discussion (6 min) — announce "One minute to wrap up!" |
| 41:30-42:30 | Wrap-up buffer |

### During discussion — your job:

1. **Walk around.** Listen to snippets from each table.
2. **Sit with quiet tables** for 1-2 minutes. Ask: "What came up for you on this question?"
3. **Redirect dominant speakers:** "That's a great point — [name], what's your experience with this?"
4. **Collect good quotes mentally** — you'll use them in transitions later.
5. **Energy check at minute 12:** If energy is dropping, clap and say "Switch questions! Fresh topic!"

### If a table finishes early:
> "Bonus challenge: Can anyone at your table take your PARENTS' side and defend their view?"

### If the room is too loud (good problem):
Let it ride. High energy means engagement. Only intervene if one person is clearly dominating while others look bored.

### If someone shares something emotional:
Acknowledge it briefly: "Thank you for sharing that. That takes courage." Don't probe further unless they want to continue.  
/ 如果有人分享了比较感性的内容，简单认可："谢谢你的分享，这需要勇气。" 不要追问。

## Slide 9 — Useful Expressions

**Time:** 42:30-44:00 (1.5 minutes)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 9. Brief language support interlude.

> **SAY:**  
> "Quick language break! If you felt stuck finding words in Round 1, here are some phrases you can steal for Round 2."

*Read 2-3 key phrases aloud:*

> "'My parents grew up in a completely different era' — simple but powerful."  
> "'They come from a place of love, but it feels like pressure' — this is a great way to acknowledge both sides."  
> "'It's not that they're wrong — we just have different priorities' — diplomatic and honest."

> "Use these in Round 2. Or make up your own. Remember — don't aim for perfect English. Aim for a precise thought."

**Tip:** Don't read every expression. Pick 2-3 that feel natural. This is a breathing moment between rounds.  
/ 不用全读。挑2-3个自然的。这是两轮之间的喘息时间。

## Slide 10 — The Stakes

**Time:** 44:00-47:00 (3 minutes)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 10. Lower your voice slightly — shift to a more serious register.

> **SAY:**  
> "Before we go into Round 2, I want to raise the stakes a bit. Because this isn't just about fun dinner table arguments."

*Pause.*

> "When the generation gap goes unaddressed — when people stop trying to communicate — real things break."

*Point to the three cards:*

> "First: **Family Estrangement.** A 2023 survey found that 31% of Chinese young adults say they actively avoid deep conversations with their parents. Not out of rebellion — out of exhaustion. They've given up trying to be understood."

> "Second: **Mental Health.** 67% of Gen Z respondents say parental pressure around marriage and career is their NUMBER ONE stressor. Not work. Not money. Their parents."

> "And third: **The Loneliness Loop.** Parents feel unneeded. Children feel misunderstood. Both sides withdraw. The gap becomes a wall — and nobody built it on purpose."

*Pause.*

> "So Round 2 is going to ask bigger questions. Not just 'what's the gap?' but 'what do we DO about it?'"

**Tip:** This is a pivot moment. The room should feel the weight shift from light sharing to meaningful inquiry. Don't rush. Let silence do some work.  
/ 这是转折点。让房间感受到从轻松分享到深度探讨的重量转移。不要急。让沉默帮你。

## Slide 11 — Round 2 Title

**Time:** 47:00-47:30 (30 seconds)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 11.

> **SAY:**  
> "Round 2 — 'The Bigger Picture.' Twenty-five minutes. Three questions. And here's what's different: I'd like you to switch tables. Mix it up. Find people you haven't talked to yet."

*Wait for people to move. This takes 30-60 seconds.*

> "New faces, new perspectives. Let's go."

**Tip:** If people resist moving, make it fun: "Find someone wearing a different color from you" or "Move to the table furthest from where you are now."  
/ 如果大家不想动，加点趣味性："找一个跟你穿不同颜色衣服的人坐。"

## Slide 12 — Round 2 Q1: Is the Gap Wider Than Ever?

**Time:** 47:30-55:30 (8 minutes)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 12.

> **SAY:**  
> "Question 1 for Round 2: 'Is the generation gap wider than ever — or is it the same as it's always been?'"

> "Look at the two sides on screen. On the left: arguments that it's WIDER than ever — technology, China's rapid economic change, social media creating separate worlds. On the right: arguments that it's the SAME — parents have always worried, children have always rebelled, Socrates was complaining about youth in 400 BC."

> "Here's the twist: I want you to **defend the side you DISAGREE with** for at least two minutes. If you think it's wider, argue that it's the same. If you think it's the same, argue that it's wider."

> "Why? Because understanding the other side is literally the skill we need to bridge any gap. Start now — 8 minutes."

**Facilitation:**
- At the 4-minute mark, say: "OK, now switch to your REAL opinion. What do you actually think?"
- This is a mini-debate format. If tables are struggling, prompt them: "What evidence would change your mind?"

## Slide 13 — Round 2 Q2: Whose Job Is It?

**Time:** 55:30-65:30 (10 minutes)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 13.

> **SAY:**  
> "Question 2 — and this one's a role-play. 'Whose job is it to bridge the generation gap?'"

> "I need four groups. If you have four tables, perfect — one role per table. If not, split your table in half."

*Assign roles:*

> "Table 1 — you are PARENTS. Your position: 'We sacrificed everything for you.'"  
> "Table 2 — you are YOUNG ADULTS. Your position: 'Let me live my own life.'"  
> "Table 3 — you are EDUCATION. Your position: 'We shape the next generation.'"  
> "Table 4 — you are MEDIA & CULTURE. Your position: 'We tell the stories.'"

> "You have 4 minutes to prepare your argument. Then we'll do a quick cross-debate — each group gets 90 seconds to present."

### Timing:

| Time | Action |
|------|--------|
| 55:30-59:30 | Prep time (4 min) — walk around, help groups who are stuck |
| 59:30-61:00 | Group 1 presents (90 sec) |
| 61:00-62:30 | Group 2 presents (90 sec) |
| 62:30-64:00 | Group 3 presents (90 sec) |
| 64:00-65:30 | Group 4 presents (90 sec) |

> After all present: "OK — quick show of hands. After hearing all four, who do you think has the MOST responsibility? Parents? Young adults? Education? Media?"

**Tip:** This is the most structured activity. Keep time tight. If a group goes over 90 seconds, gently say "Thank you! Let's hear from the next group."  
/ 这是最结构化的活动。时间要卡紧。超过90秒就温柔打断。

**If groups are uneven:** Combine Education and Media into one group. Three groups works fine.

## Slide 14 — Round 2 Q3: What Will YOU Do Differently?

**Time:** 65:30-72:30 (7 minutes)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 14. Shift to a reflective, personal tone.

> **SAY:**  
> "Last question of Round 2. And this one's personal."

> "One day — maybe sooner than you think — you'll be the 'older generation.' Your kids will roll their eyes at you. They'll think your music is old, your technology is outdated, your values are... irrelevant."

*Pause for effect.*

> "So: what will you do DIFFERENTLY from your parents? And — be honest with yourself — what will you probably REPEAT?"

*Point to the prompts on screen:*

> "Think about these: Will you pressure your kids about marriage? Will you try to understand technology that feels alien to you? Will you give unsolicited advice? Will you respect choices you don't understand?"

> "And here's the sentence I want everyone to complete before we finish: **'When I'm a parent, I will ___. But I'll probably also ___.'"**

> "Share with your table. 5 minutes. Then I'll ask 2-3 volunteers to share with the whole room."

### At 70:30 (2 minutes before end):

> "OK — who wants to share their sentence with the whole room? 'When I'm a parent, I will ___. But I'll probably also ___.'"

*Take 2-3 volunteers. Thank each one warmly.*

**Tip:** This question lands best when you go first. Share YOUR answer honestly — especially the "But I'll probably also..." part. Vulnerability creates permission.  
/ 你先说自己的答案效果最好。尤其要把"但我可能也会..."那部分说得真诚。示范脆弱给别人许可。

## Slide 15 — Eight Open Cards (Flip Cards)

**Time:** 72:30-82:30 (10 minutes)  
**Action:** Click to Slide 15. Energy should be playful and lighter now.

> **SAY:**  
> "OK — final activity! These are eight mystery questions hiding behind eight desserts. I'll tap one, read the question aloud, and then someone answers — then that person picks the next card."

*Tap the first card yourself. Read the question aloud in English AND Chinese.*

> "Who wants to answer this one? ... Great! [Name], after you answer, tap the next card for us."

### The 8 questions (for your reference — they appear randomly on screen):

1. "What is one thing your parents taught you that you will keep forever — even if you rejected it at first?"
2. "If your parents were your age today, do you think they would make the same life choices?"
3. "What is something your generation does that will probably confuse the NEXT generation?"
4. "Is there a moment when you suddenly understood why your parents did something you used to hate?"
5. "If you could send a one-sentence message back to your parents when they were 25, what would you say?"
6. "What is one belief you hold today that your future grandchildren will probably laugh at?"
7. "Does technology bring generations closer together, or push them further apart?"
8. "What is something you secretly agree with your parents about — but would never admit to them?"

### Facilitation:

- You don't need to do all 8. Aim for 5-6 depending on time and energy.
- If someone gives a short answer, probe gently: "Can you tell us more about that?"
- If the energy is high and people are laughing/sharing, let it flow. Don't force all 8.
- If energy is dropping, pick the most provocative remaining card yourself.
- / 不需要全部翻完。根据时间和能量做5-6个即可。

**Handling a dominant participant:** If one person keeps volunteering, say "Let's hear from someone who hasn't shared yet. Who's been thinking quietly?"

> **SAY:**  
> "Let me close with something that puts all of tonight into perspective."

*Read the quote:*

> "'The children now love luxury; they have bad manners, contempt for authority; they show disrespect for elders. Children are now tyrants, not the servants of their households.' — attributed to Socrates, around 400 BC."

*Pause. Let people react.*

> "Twenty-four hundred years ago. The same complaint. The gap has ALWAYS existed."

> "What's new is our power to either widen it — or bridge it — with how we choose to listen."

*Look around the room:*

> "Tonight, you practiced that. You listened to experiences different from your own. You tried to see through someone else's eyes. That IS the bridge."

> "感谢你今晚的真诚分享。理解，从对话开始。Thank you for your honest sharing tonight. Understanding starts with conversation."

## Troubleshooting Common Scenarios

### "Nobody is talking"
- Ask a direct, easy question to a specific person: "What about you — do your parents understand your job?"
- Share something personal and slightly vulnerable yourself first.
- Reduce group size: "Turn to just ONE person and share."
- / 指名问一个简单的问题。或者先分享你自己的故事。

### "One person won't stop talking"
- Validate briefly: "Great point!" then redirect: "I'd love to hear from others on this."
- Use the structure: "Let's go around the table — each person gets 60 seconds."
- In extreme cases, approach privately during a transition: "You have great energy! Can you help me draw out the quieter folks?"

### "People are only speaking Chinese"
- Don't police language. Instead, encourage: "Can you try to say that key idea in English? Even just one sentence?"
- Offer scaffolding: "The phrase for that might be '___.' Want to try using it?"
- Remind them: "It doesn't have to be perfect. We're here to practice."
- / 不要当语言警察。鼓励他们把核心想法用英语说出来。哪怕只有一句话。

### "Someone gets emotional"
- Acknowledge: "Thank you for sharing that. It clearly means a lot to you."
- Don't try to fix or counsel. Just hold space.
- Move on gently after a beat: "Does anyone else relate to that feeling?"

### "We're running out of time"
- Cut Slide 9 (expressions) — people can read it themselves.
- Reduce Round 2 Q2 (role-play) from 10 min to 6 min — skip the presentation phase, just discuss.
- Do only 4-5 flip cards instead of 8.
- Never cut the closing quote (Slide 16) — it provides emotional closure.

### "We have extra time"
- Extend flip cards — do all 8 with follow-up questions.
- Ask the room: "What's one thing you're going to do differently after tonight?"
- Open floor: "Any question you wish we'd asked tonight?"
